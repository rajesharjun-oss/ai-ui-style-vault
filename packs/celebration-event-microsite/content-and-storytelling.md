# Content and Storytelling Standard

## Purpose

A celebration microsite should feel written for real people and a real occasion. The content system must preserve emotional specificity while helping guests find practical information quickly.

## Content hierarchy

Guests should be able to answer these questions without reading a long story:

1. Who is celebrating?
2. What is the occasion?
3. When and where is it happening?
4. Which events am I invited to?
5. What should I wear or bring?
6. What must I do next?

The homepage summarises. Dedicated pages carry biographies, the full story, travel details, FAQs and galleries.

## Required content classification

Every fact, quotation and story paragraph must be classified as one of:

- `verified-couple-content`
- `requires-couple-confirmation`
- `private-do-not-index`
- `prototype-only`

Do not invent:

- Names or preferred name order.
- Event dates, times, venues or addresses.
- Family names or roles.
- Proposal, faith or relationship history.
- Guest policies.
- Dress-code colours.
- Bank details or registry information.
- Travel, accommodation or shuttle arrangements.

Unknown information must be omitted, marked for confirmation or represented by a clearly labelled placeholder only in planning files—not in the finished public interface.

## Recommended copy budgets

| Element | Recommended limit |
|---|---:|
| Hero eyebrow | 2–6 words |
| Couple or event headline | 2–12 words |
| Hero support | 12–35 words |
| Primary action | 1–5 words |
| Event-card summary | 8–28 words |
| Couple profile introduction | 25–60 words |
| Timeline summary | 15–45 words |
| FAQ answer | 20–90 words |
| RSVP helper text | 8–30 words |
| Post-event thank-you intro | 20–70 words |

These limits are guidance. Meaning and cultural accuracy take priority, but long content must be deliberately structured.

## Couple profiles

Use this sequence:

1. Name and preferred title.
2. Short human introduction.
3. Three to five verified quick facts.
4. A personal quotation when supplied.
5. Expandable full biography.

Avoid mirrored filler. Each partner can have a different profile structure if that represents them better.

## Story timeline

Each milestone should contain:

- A verified date or relative sequence.
- A concise title.
- A short summary.
- Optional expanded narrative.
- Optional photograph, video or quotation with provenance.

Do not make the timeline understandable only through left/right placement or animation. Mobile uses one clear chronological column.

## Event details

Practical information must use plain language. Always distinguish:

- Ceremony from reception.
- Traditional, civil, religious and after-party events.
- Events open to all guests from invitation-specific events.
- Confirmed details from provisional arrangements.
- Local event time from the guest's device timezone.

Use explicit date, time and timezone values. “Saturday evening” may support the date, but it does not replace it.

## Dress-code content

Dress-code guidance must include words, not only swatches or photographs. Name every colour and explain formality, traditional attire, restricted colours and footwear or venue considerations when confirmed.

Do not use third-party fashion photography without permission. Generated outfit concepts must be labelled as inspiration rather than official required attire.

## RSVP language

RSVP content should be respectful and direct:

- Identify the invited guest or household.
- State the deadline.
- Explain approved guest count.
- Ask only questions necessary for planning.
- Explain how the information will be used.
- Confirm what was saved.

Do not imply that a response was received until the source of truth confirms it.

## Gifting language

Gifting must remain optional. Prefer language such as:

> Your presence is the greatest gift. For guests who have asked, the couple's verified gift options are available below.

Avoid pressure, public ranking, manipulative countdowns or unsupported payment claims.

## Cultural and faith content

- Ask for preferred terminology and spelling.
- Preserve diacritics and names accurately.
- Confirm scripture, Qur'anic verses, prayers, songs and translations.
- Do not use sacred text decoratively or invent quotations.
- Do not combine unrelated cultural motifs because they look festive.
- Let attire, ceremony, language, venue and family practices inform the design.

## Homepage compression pass

Before handoff, remove:

- Full biographies.
- Repeated countdowns.
- Repeated RSVP prompts.
- Repeated date and venue paragraphs.
- Generic statements about love that could belong to any couple.
- Long vendor acknowledgements before the event.
- Decorative sections with no guest or storytelling purpose.

## Post-event content

After the event, replace urgency with gratitude. Define:

- Thank-you message.
- Whether official media is available or coming later.
- Whether guest uploads are accepted.
- Whether the RSVP and gifting sections are hidden, closed or archived.
- How long the site and guest information will remain available.

## Reject conditions

Reject the content when:

- The site could be relabelled for another couple without meaningful change.
- Important guest questions are buried beneath romance copy.
- The first viewport is an essay.
- Personal claims were inferred from photographs or social profiles without confirmation.
- The same message is repeated across several sections.
- Long biographies or timeline chapters have no summaries or disclosure controls.
- Private details appear in public metadata, source code or analytics events.
