# Semantic Relevance Standard for 3D, Video and Animated Backgrounds

## Hard rule

Every major 3D object, scene, background animation, video loop, particle system and spatial environment must directly support the actual business, product, service, process, place, audience or page purpose. **Visual impressiveness alone is not a valid reason to use an asset.**

A pizza website must not receive a Ferrari merely because the model is attractive. A tax firm must not receive a spaceship because it looks futuristic. A wedding site must not receive an unrelated cyber city because it looks premium.

## Relevance test

Before an asset enters the build, answer:

1. What real business/product/service/page fact does this subject represent?
2. Which relationship applies: product, service, process, place, brand symbol, verified data or audience context?
3. Would the asset still make sense if the brand name and logo were removed?
4. Is the subject accurate, verified and non-misleading?
5. Is there a more relevant asset or a simpler medium?

If questions 1–4 cannot be answered specifically, reject the asset.

## Relevance score

Score every major visual from 0–5:

- 5 — the actual product/place/process or a precise visualisation of verified data.
- 4 — directly supports a core service, use case or brand-specific concept.
- 3 — relevant supporting atmosphere or material metaphor; acceptable only as secondary decoration.
- 2 — loosely category-related but generic; do not use as a primary visual.
- 1 — visually attractive but semantically weak.
- 0 — unrelated or misleading.

Primary 3D subjects and hero video/backgrounds require **4 or 5**. Secondary decoration requires **at least 3**. A score below 3 is rejected.

## Scene consistency

Relevance applies to the whole scene, not only the central model. Validate:

- Object subject.
- Environment/background.
- Props.
- Materials and textures.
- Lighting mood.
- Camera behaviour.
- Particle effects.
- Sound, if any.
- Video or fallback imagery.

A relevant pizza model inside an unrelated luxury-car showroom is still a failed scene.

## Business research dependency

Do not select 3D assets before the business/product research and content contract are complete. The approved subject list must be derived from verified business facts, supplied assets, product catalogue, service description and page purpose.

## Generated media

Generated objects and backgrounds must not invent products, facilities, properties, awards, capabilities or physical details. Label conceptual decorative media internally and never present it as documentary evidence.

## Asset source selection

Search asset libraries only after the approved-subject list is written. Search by the approved nouns and attributes, not by broad terms such as “cool 3D”, “premium 3D” or “futuristic background”.

## Visual QA

During rendered QA ask:

- Does every major object belong to this business?
- Does every background support the section claim?
- Are environment, lighting and material choices consistent with the brand/product?
- Is any asset present only because it looks expensive or impressive?
- Could the scene be moved unchanged to an unrelated website? If yes, revise it.

The build is incomplete until all major visual assets pass the relevance gate.
