# Fashion & Couture Photography and Material Standard

## Priority order

1. User/owner-supplied official garment and atelier photography.
2. Newly commissioned or owned brand photography.
3. Clearly licensed editorial/product photography when appropriate.
4. Generated concept imagery only for prototyping or clearly provisional presentation.

## Garment coverage

For important garments or categories, aim for a coherent set of: full silhouette, front/three-quarter view, construction/detail crop, fabric/texture macro and context/occasion image where available. Do not use only dramatic close-ups when fit is the purchase decision.

## Art direction

Keep lighting, background, crop, colour treatment and camera height consistent within a collection. Corporate tailoring should preserve line, fit, lapel/collar, sleeve and trouser/skirt proportion. Traditional wear and kaftans should preserve embroidery, drape, textile texture and culturally specific details without generic motifs.

## Provenance

Record source, ownership/licence, subject, crop, intended use and whether the image is official, owner-supplied, licensed or generated. Never imply generated people are clients, models hired by the business or the owner. Never imply a generated garment is an actual finished EmmytainAttain-style product or another brand's work.

## 3D and motion assets

A 3D garment, mannequin, fabric simulation, sewing tool, pattern, thread or material surface must pass the 3D semantic relevance contract. Prefer verified garment geometry or abstract material/fabric motion over fake exact replicas of real products. Static photography remains the fallback.
