# Fashion & Couture Content and Merchandising

The site must explain what the atelier makes, for whom, how custom work happens, why the craftsmanship is credible and how a client should enquire. Do not substitute generic luxury language for product facts.

## Required hierarchy

1. Specific offer and audience.
2. Core garment/service categories.
3. Craft, fit and material evidence.
4. Lookbook or portfolio with truthful provenance.
5. Bespoke/order process.
6. Verified trust signals.
7. Primary enquiry or booking channel.

## Copy rules

- Name real categories: corporate suits, kaftans, traditional wear, bridal, occasion wear, uniforms, alterations or whatever research verifies.
- Do not invent prices, fabric origins, turnaround times, delivery areas, client lists, celebrities, awards or measurements.
- Use premium language sparingly. Prefer concrete statements about cut, fit, construction, fabric sourcing, finishing and process when verified.
- Keep hero support copy concise. Let garment imagery, material detail and process evidence carry visual weight.
- A slogan may support the brand, but it must not replace a specific value proposition.
- If pricing is quote-based, say so only when verified and make the enquiry path explicit.

## Merchandising rules

- Organise by the way clients actually shop or commission work: garment type, occasion, gender/audience, collection or service category.
- Do not fabricate SKUs for a bespoke-only business.
- Separate ready-to-wear availability from made-to-order work.
- Show material/fabric information only when the source is verified or owner-confirmed.
- A portfolio item must be labelled as official work, owner-supplied work, licensed editorial media or generated concept media internally; generated concept media cannot be presented as completed client work.
