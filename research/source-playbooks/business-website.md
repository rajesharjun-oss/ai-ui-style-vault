# Business Website Source Playbook

Use this playbook when the source is the business's own public website.

## Objective

Treat the site as a structured evidence source for business identity, offers, conversion, operations and owned media while avoiding accidental copying of protected design expression.

## Inspect

Check the homepage plus only the pages needed to establish the business: about, services/products, contact/location, pricing, booking/order, FAQ/policies and evidence/proof pages when available.

Capture exact source values for:

- official business/brand name;
- contact details and addresses;
- named services/products and their source wording where necessary;
- prices, durations, availability or policies only when explicitly published;
- primary actions and their actual destinations;
- branch/location information;
- stated credentials, certifications, partners, guarantees or customer proof only when visible and attributable;
- operational terms that materially affect the build.

## Design and content separation

The website is evidence for the business, not a layout to clone. Record palette, type character, density, photographic subject matter, content hierarchy and interaction expectations as abstract `brandSignals`. Do not copy exact page composition, source code, proprietary text beyond needed factual values, motion choreography, illustrations or visual assets without permission/licence.

## Assets

Determine whether media is owned/usable before adding it to the asset manifest. A publicly visible image is not automatically licensed for reuse. When ownership/permission is uncertain, keep it as research evidence and mark the build asset status accordingly.

Do not silently replace an inaccessible official image with a visually similar stock image while presenting it as business media.

## Products, services and pricing

Preserve exact names/prices when the build uses them. Do not infer a price from a package, calculate discounts not stated by the source, merge separate services into a new offer, or invent product variants.

If content appears stale or contradictory across pages, record the conflict and lower confidence instead of choosing whichever version is convenient.

## Forms and conversion

Inspect what the existing CTA actually does: navigate, email, phone, WhatsApp, external booking, cart/checkout, lead form, account flow, etc. A visual button is not proof that a backend workflow is functioning; distinguish published intent from verified functional capability.

## Blocked/partial access

If important pages or media are unavailable, record the gap. Search other public/owner-provided sources only as separate evidence records. Do not claim the missing content was inspected.

## Default asset stance

For redesigns, prefer owner-supplied/owned assets and an explicit reuse decision. For new builds, choose `allowlist-only`, `verified-only`, or `verified-plus-generated-concept` based on actual permission and user intent.
